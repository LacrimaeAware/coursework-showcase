A circular target for clock time

Assignment 6 predicts quantities from images. Its first task reads synthetic clock faces. There are \(720\) possible minute labels in a twelve-hour cycle, represented by \(96\times96\) grayscale images.

Clock time wraps around: \(11{:}59\) and \(12{:}00\) are one minute apart. The model therefore predicts a point on a circle rather than a single position on a line. For minute label \(m\), the two target coordinates are

\[ \left(\sin\frac{2\pi m}{720},\; \cos\frac{2\pi m}{720}\right). \]

A four-block PyTorch CNN learns those coordinates. Its final layer has two outputs; the predicted angle is converted back to minutes with the two-argument arctangent. Training uses mean squared error between the predicted and target coordinates.

Evaluation measures the shorter distance around the clock. If \(d\) is the absolute difference between true and predicted minutes, the error is \(\min(d,720-d)\). Across the \(108\) test images, the recorded mean error is \(4.5\) minutes and the median is \(3.7\) minutes. The circular target and circular error express the same underlying geometry.

A scalar target for triangle count

The second task predicts how many triangles appear in a synthetic line drawing. It uses \(840\) images with counts from \(0\) to \(20\). A separate three-block CNN predicts a single normalized count, which is converted back to triangle units for evaluation.

The mean test error is \(0.93\) triangles and the median is \(0.84\). Overlapping outlines make some examples harder to count. The two tasks show why output representation and evaluation units should follow the quantity being predicted.
