Reaction clocks

The enzyme mechanism can be simulated as a sequence of random events. The current molecule counts set the binding, complex-separation and product-formation event rates.

Two stochastic sampling methods
MethodClock and outcome
Fixed time stepAdvance by a small interval, then select binding, separation, product formation or no reaction.
Exact Gillespie SSASample the waiting time and next reaction from the current event rates.

The comparison below uses the exact stochastic simulation algorithm (SSA). Repeated runs show how random product counts relate to the deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE).

Continuous rates and reaction events

An enzyme binds substrate to form a complex. The complex can separate again or form product, freeing the enzyme for another reaction.

\[E+S\;\rightleftharpoons\;\mathrm{ES}\;\longrightarrow\;E+P\]

\(E\): free enzyme · \(S\): substrate · \(\mathrm{ES}\): complex · \(P\): product

Product across repeated runs

The orange path shows product counts in one stochastic run. The teal curve averages the simulated runs, and the shading spans the middle \(95\%\) of product counts at each time. The dashed curve is the deterministic mass-action approximation, computed with ordinary differential equations (ODEs).

Differential equations and source code

Mass-action differential equations
\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dE}{dt}&=-c_1 E\,S+(c_2+c_3)\mathrm{ES}\\ \frac{dS}{dt}&=-c_1 E\,S+c_2\mathrm{ES}\\ \frac{d\,\mathrm{ES}}{dt}&=c_1 E\,S-(c_2+c_3)\mathrm{ES}\\ \frac{dP}{dt}&=c_3\mathrm{ES} \end{aligned}\]

Free enzyme plus complex stays constant. Substrate plus complex plus product also stays constant, expressing the two molecule balances.
