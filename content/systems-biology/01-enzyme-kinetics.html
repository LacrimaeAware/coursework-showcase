Reaction rates and molecule balances

The model follows an enzyme \(E\), substrate \(S\), bound complex \(\mathrm{ES}\), and product \(P\). Binding joins enzyme and substrate. The complex can separate again or release product, leaving the enzyme available for another reaction.

\[E+S\;\underset{k_2}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}}\;\mathrm{ES}\;\xrightarrow{k_3}\;E+P\]

The reaction rates are \(v_1=k_1 E\,S\) for binding, \(v_2=k_2\mathrm{ES}\) for separation, and \(v_3=k_3\mathrm{ES}\) for product formation. In the first expression, \(E\,S\) multiplies the free enzyme and substrate concentrations. The name \(\mathrm{ES}\) denotes the bound complex.

The MATLAB baseline starts with \(E_0=1\), \(S_0=100\), and \(\mathrm{ES}(0)=P(0)=0\), with \(k_1=k_2=k_3=1\). It integrates the four changing concentrations over \(0\leq t\leq140\). Adding their gains and losses gives two conserved totals: \(E+\mathrm{ES}=1\) and \(S+\mathrm{ES}+P=100\).

The function ode15s calculates the concentration trajectories from their derivatives. At each solver evaluation, the model computes the reaction rates from the current concentrations, then adds each species' gains and losses.

Michaelis–Menten saturation

After the complex's initial adjustment, its formation and loss can nearly balance. This quasi-steady-state assumption sets \(d\mathrm{ES}/dt\approx0\) while substrate and product continue changing. Combining that balance with the fixed total enzyme gives the reduced reaction rate

\[v=\frac{V_{\max}S}{K_m+S}.\]

Here \(v=dP/dt\), \(K_m=(k_2+k_3)/k_1\), and \(V_{\max}=k_3E_0\). The Michaelis constant \(K_m\) is the substrate concentration at half the maximum rate \(V_{\max}\). The baseline gives \(K_m=2\) and \(V_{\max}=1\). At \(S=2\), the rate is \(1/2\). As substrate increases, the rate approaches \(1\) because most enzyme is already occupied.

Four concentration equations
\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dE}{dt}&=-v_1+v_2+v_3,\\ \frac{dS}{dt}&=-v_1+v_2,\\ \frac{d\mathrm{ES}}{dt}&=v_1-v_2-v_3,\\ \frac{dP}{dt}&=v_3. \end{aligned}\]

The rate constants stay fixed during the calculation. Reaction rates change as the free and bound concentrations change.
