Initial conditions and their destinations

At fixed external TMG \(T=10\,\mathrm{\mu M}\), the two initial state values vary across a grid. Each point starts a trajectory, which is classified by the stable state it approaches. A basin of attraction is the collection of starting states that approach the same destination.

The fixed-input lac equations

The state variables are normalized internal TMG \(X\) and LacY expression \(Y\). Initial values \(X(0)\) and \(Y(0)\) determine the point plotted on the map. Both evolve according to the classroom model:

\[ \frac{dX}{dt}=\beta Y-X \] \[ \frac{dY}{dt}=\alpha\frac{1+X^2}{\rho+X^2}-Y, \] \[ \alpha=100,\quad \rho=167.1. \] \[ \beta=0.123T^{0.6},\quad T=10. \]

Each grid point supplies a separate initial condition. The solver advances that trajectory, then compares its final position and remaining rate of change with the stable states. Black marks the lower destination and red the upper destination; gray is reserved for runs that remain unresolved within the integration limits.

On the saved grid, \(X(0)\) and \(Y(0)\) each range from \(0\) to \(10\). There are \(1040\) low-state destinations and \(1561\) high-state destinations. The finer samples follow the transition between those two groups.

The low equilibrium is approximately \((0.324,0.661)\), the intermediate equilibrium \((3.341,6.823)\), and the high equilibrium \((45.303,92.516)\). Initial points in the displayed square can therefore evolve to a stable state beyond its plotted extent. The intermediate equilibrium is a saddle: deviations in one direction grow while deviations along another direction decay.
