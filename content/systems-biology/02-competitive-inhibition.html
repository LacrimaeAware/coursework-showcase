Competition for free enzyme

Competitive inhibition adds a molecule \(I\) that binds free enzyme to form an inhibitor-bound complex \(\mathrm{EI}\). Substrate and inhibitor therefore share the available enzyme. Product forms from the substrate-bound complex \(\mathrm{ES}\), so enzyme held in \(\mathrm{EI}\) slows product accumulation.

\[\begin{gathered}E+S\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{ES}\longrightarrow E+P\\E+I\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{EI}\end{gathered}\]

The Python comparison tracks six changing quantities: \(E,S,\mathrm{ES},P,I,\mathrm{EI}\). Each simulation starts with free enzyme \(E_0=1\), substrate \(S_0=10\), and zero bound complexes or product. Four initial inhibitor levels, \(I_0=0,0.5,2,10\), are compared over the same time interval.

Product forms more slowly

At \(t=10\), the numerical summary gives \(P\approx7.11\) for \(I_0=0\) and \(P\approx2.94\) for \(I_0=10\). Reaching \(P=5\) takes approximately \(6.65\) and \(19.30\) time units, respectively. Increasing inhibitor delays conversion while the initial enzyme, substrate, and reaction constants stay fixed.

The constants in this example are illustrative: substrate binding, complex separation, product formation, inhibitor binding, and inhibitor release all use rate-constant value \(1\).

Free, substrate-bound, and inhibitor-bound enzyme

The partition plot connects the slower product curve to enzyme occupancy. As substrate is consumed, less enzyme remains in \(\mathrm{ES}\); more becomes free or inhibitor-bound. Their sum stays constant:

\[E+\mathrm{ES}+\mathrm{EI}=E_0=1.\]

After the bound complexes adjust, their formation and loss can approximately balance. Applying this approximation to both \(\mathrm{ES}\) and \(\mathrm{EI}\) gives the competitive-inhibition rate law. Here \(I\) is free inhibitor concentration and \(K_i=k_{i,\mathrm{off}}/k_{i,\mathrm{on}}\) is the ratio of inhibitor release to binding constants. The rate is

\[v=\frac{V_{\max}S}{K_m(1+I/K_i)+S}.\]

The maximum rate \(V_{\max}=k_3E_0\) remains fixed by total enzyme and product formation; inhibitor increases the apparent Michaelis constant to \(K_m(1+I/K_i)\).
