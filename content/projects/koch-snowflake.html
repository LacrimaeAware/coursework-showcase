Refining the boundary

Start with an equilateral triangle of side length \(s\). Divide each boundary segment into thirds and replace its middle third with the other two sides of an outward equilateral triangle. Repeating that rule produces the Koch snowflake.

Every refinement replaces one segment with \(4\) segments, each \(1/3\) as long.

Perimeter at each stage

After \(n\) refinements, the boundary has \(3\cdot4^n\) segments of length \(s/3^n\). Multiplying the number of segments by their length gives

\[ P_n=3s\left(\frac43\right)^n. \]

Since \(4/3>1\), these perimeters grow without bound. The limiting boundary has infinite length even though the entire construction stays within a bounded region.

Area added by the triangles

The initial triangle has area \(A_0=\sqrt3\,s^2/4\). At refinement \(n+1\), each of the \(3\cdot4^n\) existing segments adds a triangle of side length \(s/3^{n+1}\). Their combined area is

\[ \Delta A_{n+1}=\frac{A_0}{3}\left(\frac49\right)^n. \]

The added areas form a geometric series with ratio \(4/9\). Its sum yields a finite limiting area:

\[ \begin{aligned} A_\infty&=A_0+\frac{A_0}{3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac49\right)^n\\ &=\frac85A_0=\frac{2\sqrt3}{5}s^2. \end{aligned} \]

The boundary-length sequence diverges, while the enclosed-area sequence approaches this finite value.

Dimension of the boundary

Each Koch edge consists of \(4\) scaled copies of itself, with scale factor \(1/3\). The similarity dimension \(d\), a scaling exponent for the boundary, satisfies

\[ 4\left(\frac13\right)^d=1, \] \[ d=\frac{\log4}{\log3}\approx1.262. \]

For the Koch boundary this is also its Hausdorff dimension. The exponent lies between the dimensions of a smooth curve and a planar region. The finite-area calculation concerns the enclosed region; the dimension calculation concerns its boundary.
