Balanced remainders

Homework 4 · Exercise 1, Section 2.2 Problem 7

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), with \(b\ne0\). Prove that there are unique integers \(q,r\) such that

\[ a=qb+r,\qquad -\frac{|b|}{2}<r\le\frac{|b|}{2}. \]

The quotient \(q\) counts multiples of \(b\); the remainder \(r\) is what remains after subtracting \(qb\). This interval keeps the remainder close to zero.

Existence of the remainder

The ordinary division algorithm supplies integers \(q',r'\) with

\[ a=q'b+r',\qquad 0\le r'<|b|. \]

If \(r'\le |b|/2\), choose \(q=q'\) and \(r=r'\). These already satisfy the required interval.

If \(r'>|b|/2\), subtract \(|b|\) from the remainder. Then \(r=r'-|b|\) satisfies

\[ -\frac{|b|}{2}<r<0. \]

To keep \(qb+r=a\), increase the quotient by \(1\) when \(b>0\), and decrease it by \(1\) when \(b<0\):

\[ q=\begin{cases}q'+1,&b>0,\\q'-1,&b<0.\end{cases} \]

In either case, the change in \(qb\) is \(|b|\), exactly compensating for the change in the remainder.

Uniqueness of the remainder

Suppose \(a=qb+r=q_1b+r_1\), with both remainders in the stated interval. Subtracting gives

\[ (q-q_1)b=r_1-r. \]

The half-open interval has width \(|b|\), so \(|r_1-r|<|b|\). If \(q\ne q_1\), the left side has magnitude at least \(|b|\), a contradiction. Hence \(q=q_1\), and then \(r=r_1\).

Examples of the balanced representation
\(a\)\(b\)\(q\)\(r\)
\(17\)\(5\)\(3\)\(2\)
\(18\)\(5\)\(4\)\(-2\)
\(18\)\(-5\)\(-4\)\(-2\)
\(-18\)\(5\)\(-4\)\(2\)

Common divisors and a difference

Homework 4 · Exercise 4, Section 2.3 Problem 12

For every integer \(a\) and positive integer \(n\), prove that \(\gcd(a,a+n)\) divides \(n\). In particular, prove that \(\gcd(a,a+1)=1\).

The greatest common divisor, written \(\gcd\), is the largest positive integer dividing both inputs, provided they are not both zero.

Divisibility of the difference

Let \(d=\gcd(a,a+n)\). Because \(d\mid a\) and \(d\mid(a+n)\), it divides their difference \((a+n)-a=n\). Therefore,

\[ d\mid n. \]

For \(n=1\), this positive common divisor divides \(1\). It must therefore equal \(1\), giving \(\gcd(a,a+1)=1\).
