Holding times and the ANOVA ratio

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) compares group means by separating variation between the groups from variation within them. Homework 3 applies it to \(15\) holding-time observations divided among three recordings. The null hypothesis is that all three population means are equal; the alternative allows at least one difference.

The between-group sum of squares is \(149.2\), and the within-group sum of squares is \(139.2\). Dividing by their respective degrees of freedom produces the mean squares:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{MS}_{\mathrm{between}}&=149.2/2=74.6,\\ \mathrm{MS}_{\mathrm{within}}&=139.2/12=11.6,\\ F&=74.6/11.6\approx6.431. \end{aligned} \]

The recorded \(p\)-value is \(0.0126\), evidence against equal population means under the model assumptions. It does not establish that every pair differs. In the constructed Homework 4 example, all three sample means equal \(5\); its between-group sum of squares is \(0\), giving \(F=0\) and \(p=1\).

Home-price differences and simultaneous intervals

The home-price example contains \(120\) observations, \(30\) from each of four states. Its ANOVA gives \(F=7.355\) with \(3\) and \(116\) degrees of freedom, and \(p=0.000148\). The next question is which mean differences the data support.

homework3 <- read.csv("homework3.csv")
homework3$State <- as.factor(homework3$State)
model_price <- aov(Price ~ State, data = homework3)
summary(model_price)
TukeyHSD(model_price)

as.factor() treats state as a group label. The formula Price ~ State puts price on the response side and state on the explanatory side. TukeyHSD() compares the \(\binom{4}{2}=6\) pairs with intervals designed to cover the family of mean differences together at the stated confidence level.

These three intervals are below zero, indicating lower mean prices than California in this supplied sample. The other three intervals include zero. The adjustment matters because inspecting several comparisons creates several opportunities for an incorrect rejection.

A residual is an observed price minus its fitted state mean. This plot compares standardized residuals with normal-distribution quantiles; its upper-tail departures deserve attention. ANOVA's reference calculation assumes independent observations, approximately normal errors and a common within-group variance. A small \(p\)-value does not verify those assumptions, and this observational comparison does not establish a causal effect of living in a state.
