A predictor alone and after adjustment

Homework 6 asks why newspaper advertising appears related to sales when considered alone, then contributes little evidence after radio spending is included. Both fits use the same \(200\) observations. Sales are in thousands of units, and each advertising budget is in thousands of dollars.

advertising <- read.csv("Advertising.csv")
model_simple <- lm(
  sales ~ newspaper, data = advertising
)
model_multiple <- lm(
  sales ~ newspaper + radio, data = advertising
)
summary(model_multiple)$coefficients

The first newspaper coefficient summarizes a relationship across observations with different newspaper budgets. The second compares newspaper budgets while holding radio spending fixed in the fitted model. These answer different questions: radio and newspaper spending vary together, so the newspaper-only relationship includes variation shared with radio.

A high \(p\)-value does not prove an exactly zero effect; it describes the strength of evidence for this coefficient in this particular model.

Fitted values, residuals and explained variation

Homework 7 extends the analysis to television spending and reconstructs the model summary from fitted values and residuals. For an ordinary least-squares model containing an intercept, the total variation around the response mean separates into explained and residual parts:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{SST}&=\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2,\\ \mathrm{SSR}&=\sum_i(\hat y_i-\bar y)^2,\\ \mathrm{SSE}&=\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2,\\ \mathrm{SST}&=\mathrm{SSR}+\mathrm{SSE}. \end{aligned} \] 
model_nr <- lm(
  sales ~ newspaper + radio, data = advertising
)
y <- advertising$sales
ybar <- mean(y)
yhat_nr <- fitted(model_nr)
resid_nr <- residuals(model_nr)
SST_nr <- sum((y - ybar)^2)
SSR_nr <- sum((yhat_nr - ybar)^2)
SSE_nr <- sum(resid_nr^2)

Here model_nr is the newspaper-and-radio fit. fitted() supplies its predictions and residuals() supplies observed minus predicted sales. The fraction \(\mathrm{SSR}/\mathrm{SST}\) reproduces the model's \(R^2\). The remaining fraction is variation not explained by those predictors.

The added television model uses sales ~ TV + radio + newspaper. Its overall \(F\) test asks whether the predictor slopes are jointly zero, while an individual coefficient test asks about one slope conditional on the others. Including more predictors changes the comparison; it does not automatically remove all omitted-variable problems or establish causation.
