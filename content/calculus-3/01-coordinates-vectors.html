Distance to a coordinate plane

Chapter 12.1 · Problem 3

Which of the points

\[ \begin{aligned} A&=(-4,0,-1),\\ B&=(3,1,-5),\\ C&=(2,4,6) \end{aligned} \]

is closest to the \(yz\)-plane? Which point lies in the \(xz\)-plane?

The coordinate perpendicular to the plane

The \(yz\)-plane consists of points whose \(x\)-coordinate is zero. Moving perpendicularly to it changes only \(x\), so the distance from \((x,y,z)\) to this plane is \(|x|\). The three distances are

\[ \begin{aligned} d(A,yz)&=|-4|=4,\\ d(B,yz)&=|3|=3,\\ d(C,yz)&=|2|=2. \end{aligned} \]

The smallest distance is \(2\), so \(C\) is closest to the \(yz\)-plane.

The \(xz\)-plane consists of points with \(y=0\). Only \(A=(-4,0,-1)\) has this coordinate, so \(A\) lies in the \(xz\)-plane.

Side lengths of a triangle

Chapter 12.1 · Problem 12

Find the side lengths of the triangle with vertices

\[ \begin{aligned} P&=(2,-1,0),\\ Q&=(4,1,1),\\ R&=(4,-5,4). \end{aligned} \]

Determine whether the triangle is right or isosceles. A right triangle has a right angle; an isosceles triangle has two equal side lengths.

Displacements and squared lengths

A displacement vector subtracts the starting point's coordinates from the ending point's coordinates. Its length is the square root of the sum of its squared components:

\[ \begin{aligned} \overrightarrow{PQ}&=(2,2,1),\\ |\overrightarrow{PQ}|^2&=2^2+2^2+1^2=9,\\ \overrightarrow{PR}&=(2,-4,4),\\ |\overrightarrow{PR}|^2&=2^2+(-4)^2+4^2=36,\\ \overrightarrow{QR}&=(0,-6,3),\\ |\overrightarrow{QR}|^2&=0^2+(-6)^2+3^2=45. \end{aligned} \]

The side lengths are \(3\), \(6\) and \(3\sqrt5\). The largest squared length equals the sum of the other two:

\[ 45=9+36. \]

Thus the triangle is right, with its right angle at \(P\). The two vectors from \(P\) also have dot product \(2\cdot2+2(-4)+1\cdot4=0\), confirming that they are perpendicular. All three side lengths differ, so the triangle is not isosceles.
