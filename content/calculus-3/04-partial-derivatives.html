First partial derivatives at a point

Section 14.3 · Example 1

For

\[ f(x,y)=x^3+x^2y^3-2y^2, \]

find \(f_x\), \(f_y\) and their values at \((2,1)\). A partial derivative measures change with respect to one input while the other input is held fixed.

Holding one variable fixed

For \(f_x\), the factors involving \(y\) act as constants. For \(f_y\), the factors involving \(x\) act as constants:

\[ \begin{aligned} f_x(x,y)&=3x^2+2xy^3,\\ f_y(x,y)&=3x^2y^2-4y. \end{aligned} \]

Substitution gives

\[ \begin{aligned} f_x(2,1)&=3(2)^2+2(2)(1)^3\\ &=16,\\ f_y(2,1)&=3(2)^2(1)^2-4(1)\\ &=8. \end{aligned} \]

At this point, the first value is the slope along the surface trace with \(y=1\) fixed. The second is the slope along the trace with \(x=2\) fixed.

Second and mixed partial derivatives

Section 14.3 · Example 7

Find all four second partial derivatives of

\[ f(x,y)=x^3+x^2y^3-2y^2. \]

A mixed partial differentiates with respect to both variables. Here \(f_{xy}\) means first finding \(f_x\), then differentiating that result with respect to \(y\).

Differentiating the first derivatives

Starting from \(f_x=3x^2+2xy^3\) and \(f_y=3x^2y^2-4y\), differentiation gives

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{xx}&=6x+2y^3,\\ f_{xy}&=6xy^2,\\ f_{yx}&=6xy^2,\\ f_{yy}&=6x^2y-4. \end{aligned} \]

The mixed derivatives agree: \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\). Clairaut's theorem guarantees this equality at a point when the mixed partial derivatives are continuous on a disk around that point. These derivatives are polynomials, so the required continuity holds everywhere.
