Velocity and acceleration from position

Section 13.4 · Exercise 3

A moving point has position

\[ \mathbf r(t)=\left\langle-\frac12t^2,t\right\rangle. \]

Find its velocity, acceleration and speed at \(t=2\). Velocity is the time derivative of position; acceleration is the time derivative of velocity. Speed is the magnitude of velocity.

Differentiating each component

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf v(t)&=\mathbf r'(t)=\langle-t,1\rangle,\\ \mathbf a(t)&=\mathbf v'(t)=\langle-1,0\rangle,\\ \|\mathbf v(t)\|&=\sqrt{t^2+1}. \end{aligned} \]

Substituting \(t=2\) gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf r(2)&=\langle-2,2\rangle,\\ \mathbf v(2)&=\langle-2,1\rangle,\\ \mathbf a(2)&=\langle-1,0\rangle,\\ \|\mathbf v(2)\|&=\sqrt5. \end{aligned} \]

Because \(y=t\), eliminating the parameter gives \(x=-y^2/2\), a left-opening parabola. The velocity and acceleration describe motion at the point \((-2,2)\) on that path.

Position from acceleration and initial data

Section 13.4 · Exercise 15

Find velocity and position when

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf a(t)&=\langle2,0,2t\rangle,\\ \mathbf v(0)&=\langle3,-1,0\rangle,\\ \mathbf r(0)&=\langle0,1,1\rangle. \end{aligned} \]

Velocity constants

Integrating acceleration introduces one constant for each component:

\[ \mathbf v(t) =\langle2t+C_x,C_y,t^2+C_z\rangle. \]

The prescribed velocity at \(t=0\) sets \(C_x=3\), \(C_y=-1\) and \(C_z=0\). Therefore,

\[ \mathbf v(t)=\langle2t+3,-1,t^2\rangle. \]

Position constants

Integrate this velocity, again retaining a constant in each component:

\[ \mathbf r(t) =\begin{pmatrix} t^2+3t+D_x\\ -t+D_y\\ t^3/3+D_z \end{pmatrix}. \]

The prescribed position at \(t=0\) gives \(D_x=0\), \(D_y=1\) and \(D_z=1\). The position is

\[ \mathbf r(t) =\begin{pmatrix} t^2+3t\\ 1-t\\ 1+t^3/3 \end{pmatrix}. \]

Differentiating this position returns \(\langle2t+3,-1,t^2\rangle\); differentiating once more returns \(\langle2,0,2t\rangle\). At \(t=0\), the two formulas also reproduce both given initial vectors.
